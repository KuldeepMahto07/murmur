# Market research

## Size (secondary sources; definitions vary a lot)
| Scope | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| AI transcription (global) | $4.5B (2024) → $19.2B (2034), 15.6% CAGR | Market.us, cited via [TranscribeTube](https://www.transcribetube.com/blog/ai-transcription-tool-stats) |
| AI speech-to-text tools | $3.3B (2025) → $16.4B (2035), 17.4% CAGR | Precedence Research, cited via [Pepys](https://pepys.co/blog/transcription-market-size-2026) |
| AI meeting transcription | $3.9B (2025), about 25.6% CAGR | Sonix, cited via [BrassTranscripts](https://brasstranscripts.com/blog/ai-transcription-statistics-2026-industry-data) |
| Business transcription | $3.4B (2026) → $8.6B (2033) | [Persistence Market Research](https://www.persistencemarketresearch.com/market-research/business-transcription-market.asp) |
| All transcription incl. human, US | about $30B (2024) | Grand View Research, cited via [Pepys](https://pepys.co/blog/transcription-market-size-2026) |

**Takeaway:** a large, fast-growing market where AI keeps taking share from human transcription. Prices are racing down (TurboScribe offers unlimited use for $10/month), so **competing on price per minute is a losing game**. Compete on a different axis: privacy and ownership.

## Serviceable slice for Murmur (bottom-up, assumption to validate)
- Qualitative researchers: about 1M+ graduate students and faculty worldwide doing interview studies.
- Freelance journalists, podcasters, legal and HR professionals who need offline work: hundreds of thousands.
- Assume 1% of 1M reachable Windows users buy a $49 license: about **$490k** in one-time revenue, plus team or upgrade revenue. That's enough for a solo or indie business, and not venture scale. Plan accordingly.

## Trends that help us
- Open models keep getting better and smaller (Whisper large-v3-turbo runs in about 2 GB VRAM; the 42 s test took 6 s on a GTX 1650 Ti).
- Rules are tightening (EU AI Act, GDPR enforcement, institutional AI policies), and "processed on device" is becoming a procurement checkbox.
